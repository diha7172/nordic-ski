The Capability Gap

An essential part of Nordic skiing is prepping your skis for challenging conditions. In order to do this, klister wax must be applied to skis to provide optimal grip and preserve glide. The application process can be messy, time-consuming, and highly inconsistent — which is why the Nordic Ski Club has trusted us with developing a product to simplify this critical process, making the sport more accessible and enjoyable for every member.

02

Root Issues

The
Problems

Why klister wax needs a better solution

Problem Statement

What Makes
Klister Difficult?

Klister is a tacky wax applied to the bottom of skis to provide grip for cross-country skiers. It is used for icy, wet, or slushy conditions where traditional hard wax does not work. The application process includes heating the wax and carefully applying thin layers to the middle section of the ski, known as the kick zone. This improves kick-and-glide performance in tough conditions.

Our team is creating an innovative product to address the current application process that the Nordic Ski Club has brought to our attention: Klister wax is difficult to apply because it requires careful temperature control, precise spreading, and thorough cleanup — making the process time-consuming, inconsistent, and inconvenient, especially in cold outdoor conditions.

01
🤲

Cleanliness

Klister is extremely sticky and can get all over hands, clothing, ski bags, and equipment. Once it transfers, it's nearly impossible to remove without solvents — making it one of the most frustrating aspects of the process.

02
🌡️

Temperature Control

Klister must be warmed to the right viscosity before it spreads evenly. Too cold and it won't spread; too hot and it drips or becomes unmanageably thin. Achieving the right temperature outdoors in winter is particularly challenging.

03
⏱️

Time Consumption

Current application requires heating the wax, carefully spreading thin layers, waiting for each layer to set, and then cleaning up. This multi-step process can take 15–30 minutes and is difficult to do well before a race or outing.

04
📏

Inconsistent Results

Without a precise tool, the thickness and coverage of klister application varies significantly. Uneven coverage leads to poor kick-and-glide performance, requiring the skier to re-apply and waste wax.

05
🧤

Outdoor Inconvenience

Application typically happens at trailheads or parking lots in cold weather. Wearing gloves makes precise application nearly impossible, yet bare hands lead to contamination. Current tools are not designed for outdoor use.

06
📦

Portability

Current klister comes in tubes that can leak in ski bags, contaminating gear. There is no self-contained, mess-free system that skiers can bring to the trailhead and use confidently without worrying about spills.

The Traditional Application Process

Understanding how klister is currently applied helps explain why each step creates frustration. Our design goal is to simplify or eliminate as many of these steps as possible.

01

Warm the klister tube with body heat or a small torch until pliable

02

Squeeze thin beads of wax onto the kick zone of the ski base

03

Spread evenly with a scraper or finger (major contamination risk)

04

Allow to partially set before skiing

05

Clean all tools, hands, and any contaminated surfaces after use

Our Approach

The
Solutions

Engineering a better wax application experience

Design Direction

Core Design
Principles

Our solution must address every identified pain point while remaining practical for real-world use at trailheads and race venues. We've established four guiding principles that every prototype must satisfy to be considered viable.

🧼

Zero-Contact Mess

The applicator must prevent klister from contacting hands or gear. A self-contained system keeps everything isolated until the wax hits the ski base.

🎯

Precision Application

Consistent, even coverage every time. The design should guide the skier through correct application without requiring expertise or a heating torch.

⚡

Speed & Simplicity

Application time should be under 5 minutes. Anyone — beginner or expert — should be able to use the product successfully on their first attempt.

🎿

Trail-Ready Design

Must function in cold temperatures, work with gloves on, and be compact enough to fit in a standard ski bag without risk of leakage.

Problem Statement

The Engineering Challenge We're Solving

How might we design a portable klister wax applicator that delivers consistent, mess-free application for cross-country skiers of all skill levels, in cold outdoor conditions, in under 5 minutes?

Design Criteria

✓ No direct hand contact with klister wax during application
✓ Functions in temperatures below 20°F (-7°C)
✓ Complete application in under 5 minutes
✓ Compatible with standard klister tubes or cartridges
✓ Fits within a standard ski bag or backpack
✓ Usable while wearing ski gloves

Prototypes &

Prototype Review

Our Concepts &
Client Response

We presented three distinct prototype concepts to the CU Boulder Nordic Ski Club during our client review session. Below is a detailed summary of client feedback, including which concepts they preferred, which they did not, and what specific improvements they requested. This feedback directly shapes our next iteration.

★ Most Preferred

Roller Applicator with Integrated Heating

Concept Description

A handheld roller device that heats klister to optimal viscosity via a small integrated heating coil, then rolls it evenly onto the ski base. The wax is contained in a sealed cartridge and never touches the user's hands.

Why the Client Preferred It

The client was most excited by the integrated heating feature, noting that temperature control is their biggest frustration. They appreciated the roller mechanism for providing consistent coverage and that the cartridge system keeps everything contained. Club members specifically mentioned that "not having to touch the wax at all" was a major selling point.

Client-Requested Improvements

The client asked whether the heating element could be battery-powered and rechargeable rather than requiring an open flame. They also requested a wider roller head to cover more of the kick zone in fewer passes, and a built-in scraper on the back edge to smooth the final layer.

◈ Considered – Needs Refinement

Glove-Mounted Applicator Pad

Concept Description

A silicone applicator pad worn on the palm of the hand, pre-loaded with a measured amount of klister. The skier uses natural spreading motion to apply the wax while the silicone barrier prevents direct skin contact.

Client's Mixed Response

The client found this concept clever and appreciated its simplicity and low cost. However, they expressed concern that the spreading motion still relies heavily on hand pressure and warmth, which varies between users. They also noted it does not solve the temperature-control problem and questioned how the pad would be cleaned or replaced between uses.

Suggested Improvements

The client suggested exploring disposable or washable pad inserts. They also recommended adding a textured pattern to the pad surface to improve spreading consistency. If cost could be kept very low, they saw this as a useful complementary tool alongside the roller, but not as a standalone solution.

✕ Not Preferred

Squeeze-Tube Guided Nozzle

Concept Description

A modified nozzle attachment for standard klister tubes that features a wide, flat opening and a built-in guide rail that rests on the ski edge to maintain consistent application height and width as the tube is dragged along the ski.

Why the Client Did Not Prefer It

While the client acknowledged this was the most affordable concept, they felt it didn't meaningfully improve the existing process enough to justify adoption. The wax still needs to be spread after application, and the guide rail added bulk without solving the mess or temperature problems. One member noted, "It's just a fancier tube."

Feedback on Limitations

The client felt this prototype addressed only one of the six identified problem areas (inconsistent width) while ignoring cleanliness, temperature, speed, and portability. They encouraged us to either significantly expand its functionality or combine its guided-application concept into the roller prototype as an accessory feature.

→ Next Iteration

Refined Roller v2 — Incorporating All Feedback

How We're Moving Forward

Based on client feedback, our next prototype will be a refined version of the roller applicator. We will incorporate the rechargeable heating element, a wider roller head (50mm), an integrated edge scraper, and a guide rail inspired by Prototype C to ensure consistent application position on the ski.

Design Changes to Implement

We plan to 3D-print an updated housing with room for a USB-C rechargeable heating core, sourced through our electronics supplier. The cartridge system will be redesigned to be single-use recyclable or refillable. We will also prototype the integrated edge guide from the nozzle concept.

Testing Plan

We will test the v2 prototype at the club's next outing at Eldora Mountain. Metrics include: application time, coverage consistency, user-rated cleanliness, and ease of use for novice members. Results will be presented at our next client meeting.

Overall Client Feedback Summary

"The roller concept is exactly the kind of innovation we need."

The CU Boulder Nordic Ski Club expressed strong enthusiasm for our direction, particularly the roller applicator with integrated heating. They were most impressed by the no-contact system and the idea of cartridge-based wax delivery. Their primary requests were around making the heating self-contained, widening coverage, and ensuring the tool works in sub-zero temperatures with gloves on. They are excited to test a refined prototype at a future club outing.
