The Capability Gap

One of the most important parts of Nordic skiing is prepping your skis before you head out. Klister wax needs to be applied to the base of the ski to give you the grip you need in icy or slushy conditions. The problem is that applying it is a messy, time-consuming process that is hard to do consistently. That is why the Nordic Ski Club came to us. They wanted help building something that makes this easier for all their members.

Root Issues

The
Problems

Why klister wax needs a better solution

Problem Statement

What Makes
Klister Difficult?

Klister is a tacky wax that goes on the bottom of cross-country skis to help with grip in icy, wet, or slushy conditions where regular hard wax does not work. To apply it, you have to heat the wax first and then spread thin layers onto the middle section of the ski called the kick zone. Getting it right makes a big difference in how the ski performs.

Our team is working on a new product to fix the issues with how klister is currently applied. After talking with the Nordic Ski Club, we identified the core problem: klister wax is hard to apply correctly because you need precise temperature control, even spreading, and a full cleanup afterward. The whole thing is slow, inconsistent, and really inconvenient when you are standing outside in the cold.

01

Cleanliness

Klister is extremely sticky and gets all over your hands, clothes, ski bags, and gear. Once it gets on something, it is almost impossible to remove without a solvent. This is probably the biggest complaint we heard from club members.

02

Temperature Control

Klister has to be at just the right temperature before it spreads properly. If it is too cold it will not move. If it gets too hot it drips everywhere. Trying to get that right while standing outside in winter is really difficult.

03

Time Consumption

The current process takes anywhere from 15 to 30 minutes. You have to heat the wax, spread it in layers, wait for it to set, and then clean everything up. That is a lot of time to spend in a cold parking lot before a ski outing.

04

Inconsistent Results

Without a good tool, it is hard to get even coverage every time. If the wax is uneven, your skis will not perform as well and you end up having to scrape it off and start over, which wastes both time and wax.

05

Outdoor Inconvenience

Most people apply klister at trailheads or in parking lots. If you wear gloves you cannot spread the wax properly, but if you take them off your hands get covered in klister. None of the current tools are designed with outdoor use in mind.

06

Portability

Klister tubes can leak in ski bags and contaminate everything inside. There is no good way to carry everything you need in one contained unit that will not make a mess on the way to the trailhead.

How Klister Is Applied Right Now

Looking at the current process step by step makes it clear why skiers find it so frustrating. Our goal is to simplify as many of these steps as we can.

01

Warm the klister tube with body heat or a small torch until pliable

02

Squeeze thin beads of wax onto the kick zone of the ski base

03

Spread evenly with a scraper or finger (major contamination risk)

04

Allow to partially set before skiing

05

Clean all tools, hands, and any contaminated surfaces after use

Our Approach

The
Solutions

Engineering a better wax application experience

Design Direction

Core Design
Principles

Our solution needs to fix all the problems we identified while still being something people will actually use at a trailhead. We came up with four core principles that any prototype has to meet before we consider it a real option.

No Mess

The applicator has to keep klister away from your hands and gear completely. A self-contained system means the wax stays inside until it hits the ski base.

Precise Application

The device should make it easy to get even coverage every time. You should not need to be an experienced skier to use it correctly on your first try.

Fast and Simple

The whole process should take under 5 minutes. Whether you are a beginner or have been skiing for years, it should be straightforward to use without any learning curve.

Works Outdoors

It has to function in cold temperatures, be usable with gloves on, and fit in a standard ski bag without leaking or making a mess in transit.

Problem Statement

The Engineering Challenge We're Solving

How might we design a portable klister wax applicator that delivers consistent, mess-free application for cross-country skiers of all skill levels, in cold outdoor conditions, in under 5 minutes?

Design Criteria

✓ No direct hand contact with klister wax during application
✓ Functions in temperatures below 20°F (-7°C)
✓ Complete application in under 5 minutes
✓ Compatible with standard klister tubes or cartridges
✓ Fits within a standard ski bag or backpack
✓ Usable while wearing ski gloves

Prototype Review

Our Concepts &
Client Response

We came up with three different prototype concepts and brought them to the CU Boulder Nordic Ski Club to get their feedback. Below is a summary of what they thought about each one, what they liked, what they did not like, and what changes they asked us to make going forward.

★ Most Preferred

Roller Applicator with Integrated Heating

Concept Description

A handheld roller device with a small built-in heating coil that warms the klister to the right temperature, then rolls it evenly onto the ski base. The wax sits in a sealed cartridge so it never touches your hands at any point during the process.

Why the Client Preferred It

The client was most excited about the built-in heating because temperature control is the part they struggle with most. They liked the roller for giving consistent coverage and felt the cartridge system was a big deal since it means you never have to touch the wax. Several club members specifically said that not touching the wax at all was their favorite part of this concept.

Client-Requested Improvements

They asked if the heating element could run on a rechargeable battery instead of needing a flame. They also wanted a wider roller head so you can cover the kick zone in fewer passes, and a scraper built into the back edge to smooth out the final layer.

◈ Considered – Needs Refinement

Glove-Mounted Applicator Pad

Concept Description

A silicone pad that you wear on the palm of your hand. It comes pre-loaded with a measured amount of klister so you can spread the wax using a normal hand motion while the silicone keeps it off your skin.

Client's Mixed Response

The client thought this idea was creative and liked how simple and affordable it would be. But they had concerns about how much the results would vary from person to person since it still depends on hand pressure and body heat. They also pointed out that it does not solve the temperature problem at all, and they were not sure how you would clean it or replace it after use.

Suggested Improvements

They suggested looking into disposable or washable inserts for the pad. They also recommended adding some texture to the surface to help with more even spreading. If the cost stayed low enough, they could see it working as an add-on to the roller concept, just not as a standalone product.

✕ Not Preferred

Squeeze-Tube Guided Nozzle

Concept Description

A nozzle attachment that fits onto a standard klister tube. It has a wide flat opening and a guide rail that sits on the edge of the ski so you get a consistent bead of wax as you drag the tube along the kick zone.

Why the Client Did Not Prefer It

Even though this was the cheapest option, the client felt like it did not improve things enough to be worth switching to. You still have to spread the wax by hand after applying it, and the guide rail made the tube bulkier without fixing the mess or the temperature issues. One club member put it pretty simply: "It's just a fancier tube."

Feedback on Limitations

The client pointed out that this concept only addresses one of the six problems we identified, which is inconsistent width. It does not help with cleanliness, temperature, speed, or portability. They told us to either add a lot more features or take the guide rail idea and incorporate it into the roller as an attachment instead.

→ Next Iteration

Refined Roller v2: Incorporating All Feedback

How We're Moving Forward

Based on everything the client told us, our next prototype will be an improved version of the roller. We are going to add a rechargeable heating element, a wider 50mm roller head, a built-in edge scraper, and a guide rail attachment similar to what we tried in Prototype C.

Design Changes to Implement

We plan to 3D print a new housing that has enough room for a USB-C rechargeable heating core. The cartridge will be redesigned to be either single-use and recyclable or refillable. We will also build and test a version of the edge guide from the nozzle concept as a removable attachment.

Testing Plan

We are planning to test the updated prototype with club members at Eldora Mountain. We will track how long the application takes, how even the coverage is, how clean the process is, and how easy it is for people who have never used it before. We will bring those results back to our next client meeting.

Overall Client Feedback Summary

"The roller concept is exactly the kind of innovation we need."

Overall the CU Boulder Nordic Ski Club was really positive about where we are headed. They were most excited about the roller applicator with the built-in heating. The no-contact cartridge system stood out to them the most. Their main requests were to make the heating battery-powered, widen the roller head, and make sure the whole thing works when it is freezing outside with gloves on. They said they are looking forward to trying a new version at one of their upcoming outings.
