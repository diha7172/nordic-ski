As part of GEEN 2400: Engineering for the Community, our team is collaborating with the CU Boulder Nordic Ski Club. We are tackling the messiest problem in the sport: klister wax application.

Our integrated solution, the KlisterFixer, combines temperature control and precision mechanical compression to make waxing professional-grade, fast, and completely clean.

What We're Solving

Design Focus
Areas

Cleanliness

Direct hand contact with klister is eliminated. Our side-loading housing and sealed trigger system ensure wax stays on the ski, not on your gear.

Temp Control

Klister is notoriously temperature-sensitive. We’ve integrated an Arduino-based heating system to maintain optimal flow in sub-freezing conditions.

Consistency

The trigger mechanism uses front-pivoting compression to ensure a directed, steady flow of wax every single time you apply it.

Problem Statement

Why Klister?
And Why Now?

Klister wax is a very sticky ski wax applied to cross-country skis to provide grip in icy, wet, or slushy snow where hard wax fails[cite: 4, 5].

The Problem: Klister is difficult to apply because it requires careful temperature control, precise spreading, and thorough cleanup. This makes the process time-consuming and inconsistent, especially in cold outdoor conditions.

Our Challenge: How might we design a portable klister wax applicator that delivers consistent, mess-free application for all skill levels, in cold conditions, in under 5 minutes?.

The Chosen Concept

Our Integrated Solution

The KlisterFixer addresses every client pain point by integrating three core engineering modules into one sleek device. This side-loading housing unit combines advanced electronics with a mechanical compression system for the ultimate waxing experience.

  • Side-Loading Housing: Easy access and durable protection for the internal wax cartridges.
  • Arduino Temp Control: Real-time sensing and heating using ceramic elements.
  • Ergonomic Trigger: Oversized design compatible with thick ski gloves.

Design Iterations

Prototypes &

Prototype 01

Ergonomic
Housing

Housing Design Goals

Initially a square profile, we redesigned the housing with a rounded, ergonomic handle based on client feedback to improve grip while wearing gloves[cite: 9, 10]. We also added a protective front cap to prevent wax leakage during storage.

Prototype 02

Trigger Compression
System

Glue Gun Mechanism Inspiration

Instead of advancing a solid stick, the trigger acts as a funnel-shaped compression system that wraps around the cartridge. This front-pivoting funnel causes greater compression at the rear, creating a controlled, steady wax flow[cite: 13, 14].

Prototype 03

Circuit
Development

Smart Temp Control

Our initial design was a simple nichrome resistor connected to a battery. After consulting our client, we shifted to a smart sensing system. The revised circuit uses a temperature sensor and an Arduino to control dual-status LEDs, showing when the device is on and when it has reached optimal wax flow temperature.
